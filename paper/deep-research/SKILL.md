# Deep Research

Execute research in six stages:

1. Define objective and constraints
- Restate the user goal as a research question.
- Capture decision context, audience, depth, and required output format.
- If data is missing, make explicit assumptions and continue unless blocked.

2. Build a research plan
- Split the question into sub-questions.
- Prioritize by impact and uncertainty.
- Define evidence needed per sub-question (primary docs, standards, papers, official data, reputable reporting).

3. Gather sources
- Prefer primary sources first (official docs, filings, standards, papers, direct datasets).
- Add secondary sources for context and triangulation.
- Track publication date, author/publisher, and potential conflicts of interest.

4. Evaluate evidence quality
- Grade each source as high, medium, or low reliability.
- Check recency and identify stale evidence.
- Flag disagreements between sources instead of forcing consensus.

5. Synthesize findings
- Separate facts, inference, and speculation.
- Resolve contradictions by weighting source quality and recency.
- Identify what remains uncertain and what would reduce uncertainty.

6. Deliver structured output
- Include:
  - Scope and assumptions
  - Key findings ordered by importance
  - Evidence table mapping claims to sources
  - Risks, caveats, and open questions
  - Practical recommendation or next action

## Output Standard

For any substantial research response, provide:

- `Answer`: concise conclusion first.
- `What I checked`: domains, source types, and time windows.
- `Findings`: numbered claims with confidence labels (`high`, `medium`, `low`).
- `Conflicts or gaps`: explicit unresolved points.
- `Sources`: link each source directly and include publication date when available.

## Quality Rules

- Use exact dates when discussing time-sensitive facts.
- Never hide uncertainty; quantify confidence and explain why.
- Prefer fewer high-quality sources over many low-quality ones.
- Keep reasoning auditable: every major claim should be traceable to evidence.
- If evidence is insufficient, say so and specify what is missing.
